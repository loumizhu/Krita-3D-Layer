Krita 3D Layer — User Guide

Krita 3D Layer is an in-canvas reference tool. It gives you a lightweight 3D viewport inside a docker so you can pose models, check planes, set up perspective grids, and stamp the image directly onto your drawing layers without opening external 3D software.

Getting Started

  1. Create or open any image in Krita.
  2. Open the panel: Settings → Dockers → 3D Layer.
  3. Click Asaro Head to load the built-in reference bust, or click Import 3D to pick an .obj, .glb, or .stl file from your disk.
  4. Drag in the preview window to orbit and find the angle you want.
  5. Click Stamp to drop the current view onto a new paint layer. You can also turn on Live Sync if you want your active layer to update continuously as you move the camera.

Navigation & Shortcuts

Action Shortcut Notes
Orbit Left Click + Drag Tumble around the model.
Pan Middle Click or Alt + Left Drag Slide the view horizontally and vertically.
Zoom Right Click + Drag or Ctrl + Left Drag Move the camera closer or farther away.
Focal Length / FOV Mouse Scroll Adjust camera lens angle (telephoto vs wide-angle).
Rotate Light Shift + Left Drag Rotate the key light around the subject.
Center / Frame Double Click Resets framing and centers the model.
Resize Viewport Drag Bottom Bar Drag the handle below the 3D preview to make it larger or smaller.

Render Modes

  • Shaded Planes: Shows flat shaded polygons. Useful for value grouping, shadow mapping, and blocking in lighting.
  • Shaded + Wireframe: Combines lighting with ink wireframe edges so you can see both volume and surface flow.
  • Wireframe: Transparent background with outer and quad lines. Great for line art tracing on a layer beneath your sketch.
  • Quad Wire (Hide Diagonals): Automatically identifies and hides internal triangulation edges across quads so meshes look like clean Blender-style quad topologies.
  • Silhouette: Flat solid shape fill. Good for thumbnailing and silhouette read checks.
  • Normal Map: RGB color encoding of surface angles.

Perspective Grids

The grid section lets you set up reference planes with or without an active 3D model:

  • Ground Grid: A checkered or line-based floor grid that stays grounded under your model.
  • Ceiling Grid: An optional upper grid plane with adjustable height, helpful when sketching interior rooms or architecture.
  • Horizon Line: A reference line showing eye level. In linear perspective it stays flat; in fisheye modes it curves with the lens tilt.
  • Vertical Guides: Posts that connect floor intersections up to the ceiling for vertical alignment.

Fisheye & Curvilinear Views

Under the projection dropdown you can switch from standard perspective to spherical or fisheye lenses:

  • Equidistant / Equisolid / Stereographic: Different lens mapping styles for wide-angle and manga background distortion.
  • Curvature: Controls how strongly straight lines bend as they move toward the edges of the frame.
  • Crop Circle: Adds a circular peephole border around the lens.

Draw 3D Primitive & Perspective Inferrer

If you have sketched a box or building in your drawing and want an accurate 3D model placed in that exact perspective:

  1. Click Draw Box next to the Import button.
  2. A calibration window opens showing your canvas sketch. Drag the corner handles to outline the base and top of your drawn box, or click 4-Click Draw to tap the four corners directly.
  3. The tool instantly detects your perspective style (1-Point, 2-Point, or 3-Point), calculates the exact Field of View (FOV) and 35mm lens equivalent, determines camera tilt and box rotation, and measures the width, height, and depth proportions.
  4. Choose a primitive shape (Box, Cylinder, Sphere, or Pyramid) and click Create 3D Primitive & Calibrate Scene. The plugin creates that 3D object, matches your scene camera to your sketch, and stamps it right into place.

Lock 3D Size (FOV Calibration / Dolly Zoom)

When you enable Lock 3D Size (FOV Calibration) under the Camera & Perspective section, adjusting the Field of View slider or clicking lens buttons (e.g. 14mm, 24mm, 50mm, 85mm, 135mm) automatically compensates camera distance using optical dolly zoom. Your 3D subject maintains its exact size and framing on the canvas while perspective compression smoothly expands or flattens around it.

Ground Calibrator

If you already have a sketch or background photo and want the 3D camera to match its perspective:

  1. Click Calibrate Ground... in the docker.
  2. Move the four corner points so they line up with a known rectangular flat area in your picture (like a floor tile, rug, or table).
  3. Click Apply to Camera & Grid. The tool calculates the camera pitch, yaw, roll, and focal length to match your sketch.

Presets

When you find an angle or grid setup you want to reuse, click Save... to store it in your presets dropdown. Presets are saved locally as plain JSON:

  • Windows: %APPDATA%\krita\krita_3d_layer_presets.json
  • Linux: ~/.local/share/krita/krita_3d_layer_presets.json
  • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/krita/krita_3d_layer_presets.json

Selecting any preset from the list applies the camera, lighting, and grid values immediately.
